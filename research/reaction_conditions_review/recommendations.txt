Reaction conditions on Pourbaix diagrams: proposed revision

Evidence reviewed
The active manuscript is ../updated_stability_manuscript/submission/main.tex;
SI.tex inputs pourbaix_figures_SI.tex and the two sensitivity figure blocks.
The submission README identifies these as the submission sources.
No separate reviewer/coauthor comment text was found in the inspected sources.
This proposal addresses the reaction-window inconsistency in those sources.

The electrochemical setup (main.tex, line 361) reports 2.0 V vs RHE,
approximately 0.1 V potential uncertainty, and time-dependent pH measurements.
The captions instead call -2.0 to +2.0 V vs RHE the experimental window.
Those are different experimental claims. A broad potential screening range
must not be presented as the conditions of the constant-potential experiment.

Recommended drawing
1. Draw the nominal electrolysis potential at +2.0 V vs RHE with a black line.
2. Add a translucent green band from +1.9 to +2.1 V vs RHE. Describe this as
   the approximately 0.1 V reported potential uncertainty, not a confidence
   interval or proof that this entire range was deliberately sampled.
3. Use each electrode's measured bulk-pH interval when original numerical
   data become available. The SI pH image visually spans roughly 10.7--12.0
   across materials; these are visual estimates, not extracted measurements.
   The previews retain pH 10.5--13.5 solely as a conservative envelope.
   The actual measured bulk pH does not establish interfacial pH.
4. Existing axes are SHE. At 298.15 K use
      E_SHE = E_RHE - (2.303 R T/F) pH,
   with 2.303 R T/F approximately 0.05916 V. Existing plotters use 0.0591.
   At pH 10.5 and 13.5, the nominal line is approximately 1.379 and
   1.201 V vs SHE using the current code's prefactor. Its uncertainty edges
   are the same sloping line shifted vertically by +/-0.1 V.
   A horizontal +2 V line on an SHE axis would be incorrect.
5. If showing actual measured time points, convert each paired pH and
   measured potential, and mark the sampling times. Do not invent trajectories
   by combining independently observed extrema. If the potentiostat held a
   fixed Hg/HgO potential and RHE was calculated afterward, the RHE potential
   will vary with pH: use recorded potentials instead of assuming constant RHE.
   The methods currently need clarification on this control/conversion detail.
6. Apply the same convention to pure-metal, Pd/Pt cyanide, binary-metal,
   and SI sensitivity panels. An alternative is to transform every phase-map
   coordinate to RHE, where the +2 V line is horizontal; merely relabeling
   the existing SHE axis would be wrong.

Interpretation changes
Reassess claims at the narrow experimental band, separately from lower-
potential dissolution fields. An aqueous field encountered below +2 V does
not alone demonstrate equilibrium dissolution at the electrolysis potential.
If the band predicts an oxide but ICP-OES detects dissolution, describe this
as a limitation of the bulk-equilibrium comparison and discuss conditional
ligand chemistry, transient surface states, and kinetics as possible causes.
Do not use proximity to a lower-potential aqueous field as direct validation.
Likewise, an oxide stability field establishes a thermodynamic preference;
it does not demonstrate that a protective, continuous oxide film forms.

Keep model composition separate from the experimental overlay. The feed
is 0.1 M KOH plus 0.1 M glycine; NH3=0.02 M, CN=0 or 1e-4 M, and metal
activity=1e-4 are prescribed model conditions. They are not all measured
initial electrolyte concentrations. In particular, the cyanide case is a
conditional sensitivity scenario, not a measured cyanide exposure.

Suggested main-text replacement
"Experimental electrolysis conditions are indicated by the nominal applied
potential of 2.0 V vs RHE and its approximately 0.1 V uncertainty, converted
